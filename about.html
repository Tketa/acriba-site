About · Acriba 2024 — present

Two careers, one workshop.

A short note on how Acriba came to be, and what we are actually doing.

We are a father and a son. One of us spent thirty years producing the regulatory documents that bring drugs to patients — in medicinal chemistry, then clinical development, then at the head of European biotechs filing with the EMA and FDA. The other spent a decade building software, including one company built and sold along the way.

For years we worked in parallel, pointing in opposite directions. Then, around 2023, we started comparing notes on the same problem: the best regulatory writers were spending most of their week on work that did not require their expertise.

Eighty percent of a dossier is structure, cross-references, source traces, format. The hard, valuable twenty percent is where thirty years of experience earn their fee — and it is the part people run out of time for.

So we built Acriba around that split. A proprietary editor — AcribaWord — handles the mechanical part: ICH-compliant scaffolding, cross-references, source traces, consistency checks across the dossier. A network of senior reviewers, drawn from CXO Swiss, owns the part that decides whether an agency accepts or rejects your filing: strategy, narrative, final sign-off.

We started with regulatory writing because it is what we know. We added clinical development documents — protocols, SAPs, ICFs — because the same split applies. Strategic intelligence came next, for the same reason. We are deliberately narrow.

We work mostly with clinical-stage biotechs, the ones where one missed filing window costs a quarter and one unclear paragraph costs a meeting. You talk to the person writing your document. You know who reviewed it. You see the source trace for every sentence.

That is all we are trying to do.
